# Construction + Pascal Spatial Foundry Video Script

## Production goal

Create a clear, credible demonstration of the merged Construction + Pascal Spatial Foundry MVP without overstating what the software proves or authorizes.

Recommended finished length: **8–12 minutes**. Record the application at 1920×1080 and capture narration separately when possible. The full proof run can be shown briefly as evidence, but the narrated product demonstration should use manual Director controls.

## Recording contract

Use:

- one clean reviewed Linux checkout;
- local fixture data only;
- loopback serving only;
- no external model or live internet service during the presentation;
- no private Construction information;
- one complete fifteen-chapter Director sequence;
- exact receipts and runtime evidence from the same reviewed source tree.

Keep the exact commit and tree digest in the production notes and evidence package. Do not turn a spoken claim into a substitute for missing runtime evidence.

## Mandatory narration boundaries

Use these statements or equivalent wording:

- “This is decision support, not Construction approval.”
- “Aura retains Construction truth, evidence, authority, verification, and governance.”
- “Pascal is a pinned, disposable 2D/3D presentation organ.”
- “The as-built view is derived coordination evidence, not survey truth.”
- “The incident concerns software presentation synchronization, not physical work.”
- “Runtime verification proves this exact candidate on this exact source tree.”
- “A verified runtime result does not authorize deployment, merge, payment, access, or physical work.”
- “Human and professional authority remains outside the software demonstration.”

Do not call any export or packet:

- an approved change order;
- engineering approval;
- code certification;
- a survey drawing;
- a payment certificate;
- a work authorization;
- an automatic deployment or merge decision.

## Shot-by-shot Director sequence

The Director executes fifteen ordered chapters. The evidence profile produces seventeen named screenshots because it also captures the opening state and three separate detail views during candidate review.

### Shot 1 — Design 3D

**Director chapter:** `FRAME_CONSTRUCTION`

Action: press **Next**.

Show:

- 3D design representation;
- project/storey identity;
- Director status and chapter counter.

Narration:

> “Aura has framed the Construction objective and bound the visual presentation to the canonical project identity. The 3D view is a projection, not a second truth store.”

### Shot 2 — Floor Plan 2D

**Director chapter:** `SHOW_FLOOR_PLAN`

Action: press **Next**.

Show:

- selected floor/storey;
- Pascal node or selected element;
- visible dimensions and floor-plan controls.

Narration:

> “The floor plan is bound through the pinned Pascal artifact and the exact coordinate receipt. Storey, node, selection, and dimensions remain part of a disposable visual working copy.”

### Shot 3 — As-built evidence

**Director chapter:** `SHOW_AS_BUILT`

Action: press **Next**.

Show the Aura-derived as-built overlay.

Narration:

> “This as-built representation is derived coordination evidence. It is not a survey result, professional certification, or authorization to perform work.”

### Shot 4 — Design versus as-built

**Director chapter:** `COMPARE_REPRESENTATIONS`

Action: press **Next**.

Show the synchronized Compare view.

Narration:

> “Aura and Pascal remain separate technical owners, but their selected state is synchronized through exact receipts. The comparison helps a human inspect differences without transferring authority to the renderer.”

### Shots 5–8 — Obligations, operations, candidates, and decision packet

**Director chapter:** `REVIEW_CANDIDATES`

Action: press **Next**, then pause on the candidate review state.

Record four detail shots:

1. **Obligation inspector** — open evidence, unknowns, and required proof;
2. **Timeline, budget, and crews** — bounded coordination context;
3. **Construction candidates** — role-separated alternatives;
4. **Decision-support packet** — digest-bound summary and false authority.

Narration:

> “Aura keeps blocked options, evidence-dependent options, and ready-for-human-review options distinct. Unknown information is not converted into an invented confidence percentage. Ready for human review is not approval.”

When showing candidate roles, name:

- `HARD_BLOCKED`;
- `NEEDS_EVIDENCE`;
- `READY_FOR_HUMAN_REVIEW`.

### Shot 9 — Explicit bounded capture

**Director chapter:** `AURA_WATCH_THIS`

Action: press **Next**.

Narration:

> “Capture starts only after an explicit ‘Aura, watch this’ action. Aura does not begin unrestricted background recording. The capture is bounded to this authorized incident window.”

### Shot 10 — Deterministic incident marker

**Director chapter:** `MARK_INCIDENT`

Action: press **Next**.

Show the marked Pascal-selection synchronization fixture.

Narration:

> “The demonstration marks a deterministic presentation-interface fault: a Pascal selection acknowledgement arrives after a newer synchronized view transition. Construction state and source geometry remain unchanged.”

### Shot 11 — Replay packet

**Director chapter:** `FINALIZE_REPLAY`

Action: press **Next**.

Show the retained replay packet and capture cleanup.

Narration:

> “Before the replay is retained, active listeners, timers, buffers, and capture resources are dissolved. Aura retains a sanitized exact replay packet and required asset bindings.”

### Shot 12 — Runtime Profile V2 proof

**Director chapter:** `RUN_RUNTIME_V2`

Action: press **Next** and allow the proof to complete.

Narration before the wait:

> “Aura now delegates to the existing Runtime Profile V2 verifier. It launches the inner loopback Construction Demo profile, reproduces the software behavior, checks positive and negative requirements, preserves the source tree, runs independent verification, and terminates the runtime.”

Editing note:

- do not show the entire wait in real time;
- keep the process running;
- use a short cut, time compression, or terminal-progress insert;
- return only when the actual proof receipt is available.

Narration after completion:

> “This result is bound to the exact source tree and confirmation contract. It is evidence for human review, not permission to deploy or merge.”

### Shot 13 — Repair route

**Director chapter:** `ROUTE_REPAIR`

Action: press **Next**.

Narration:

**Director chapter:** `DEGRADED_PREVIEW`

Action: press **Next**.

Show the deliberate isolated degradation and rollback receipt.

Narration:

> “Aura deliberately demonstrates a degraded candidate in isolation, detects that the synchronization health failed, and restores the exact last-verified digest. This is technical rollback, not production hot swap.”

### Shot 15 — Successful isolated preview

**Director chapter:** `SUCCESSFUL_PREVIEW`

Action: press **Next**.

Narration:

> “The repaired candidate now passes the isolated preview. A successful preview remains a proposal and cannot deploy itself.”

### Shot 16 — Current reproof and human disposition

**Director chapter:** `CURRENT_REPROOF`

Action: press **Next**.

Show:

- P0;
- P1;
- current reproof;
- human review/disposition status;
- no automatic learning promotion.

### Shot 17 — Return, Observatory drill-down, and dissolution

**Director chapters:** `RETURN_TO_CONSTRUCTION`, then `DISSOLVE`

Action:

1. press **Next** to return to the Construction comparison view;
2. show source, receipt, identity, Pascal artifact, coordinate receipt, runtime proof, and Attempt Archive drill-down;
3. press **Next** to dissolve.

Narration:

> “The software repair lane returns to Construction without changing Construction truth. The final dissolution releases the presentation resources and retains the cleanup evidence.”

Show the dissolved status and final cleanup receipt.

## Optional relaunch ending

After dissolution, click **Restart**.

Show:

- a fresh session;
- `0/15 chapters proven`;
- no dissolved status;
- a fresh exact identity and unconsumed confirmation.

Narration:

> “Restart creates a fresh bounded session after the previous presentation has fully dissolved. It does not reuse the completed proof state.”
